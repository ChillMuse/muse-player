THE FIRST LAUNCH

One extra step,
just once.

Muse isn’t signed yet, so your computer asks you to confirm the first time you open it. After that, Muse opens like any other app.

  1. Open the downloaded file in your Downloads folder (if it’s a .zip, double-click it to unzip) and drag Muse into Applications.
  2. Open Muse from Applications. macOS says it can’t check Muse for malicious software. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security. Scroll to Security and click Open Anyway next to the message about Muse.
  4. Click Open Anyway again and enter your password. Muse opens, and from now on it opens normally.

On macOS 14 Sonoma or earlier, you can instead Control-click Muse in Applications, choose Open, then click Open.

If macOS says Muse “is damaged and can’t be opened”, open Terminal, run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Muse.app, then open Muse again.

  1. Open the setup .exe file in your Downloads folder.
  2. Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer to the end.
  4. Open Muse from the Start menu. When you close the window, Muse keeps playing from the system tray.

If your browser warns that the file isn’t commonly downloaded, choose Keep from its download menu.

THEN, IN SETTINGS

Install Smart features so Muse can choose better next songs. Go to Settings › Smart features and click Install. It’s a one-time download of about 3.6 GB, and Muse listens to your songs and reads their lyrics on your computer. Nothing leaves your computer.

Smart features need a Mac with Apple silicon or Windows. On Intel Macs, Muse plays and browses your music without them.

Not signed yet.
Still safe.

Apple and Microsoft warn about apps whose developer hasn’t bought a signing certificate. Muse is a free alpha, and its installers aren’t signed yet. The warning means the developer isn’t verified. It doesn’t mean anything harmful was found.

Muse contains no viruses or malware. Its source code is public on GitHub, and the installers are built from that code by GitHub Actions, so anyone can check what’s inside.
