Getting started.

Muse plays the music that’s already on your computer. There’s no account to make and no server to set up: Muse runs only on your computer.

  1. Download Muse for your system and open it. The installers aren’t signed yet, so the first launch needs one extra step. See how to open Muse the first time.
  2. Open Settings and add your music folders. You can add more than one.
  3. Muse scans the folders and builds your library. It scans again every time it starts, and watches the folders so new music shows up on its own.
  4. Press play. Home keeps your last track ready, so next time you can pick up where you left off.

Muse plays MP3, M4A, FLAC, WAV, OGG and Opus files. It reads titles, artists, albums and track numbers from each file’s tags.

Your library.

Home lets you resume your last track and shows the albums you added most recently.

Library has three views. Albums shows your collection by cover artwork. Artists groups your albums by artist. Songs lists every track with its artist and length. Use Search the library to find a song, album or artist by name.

When a song’s file goes missing, the song stays in the list, marked, so you can see what’s gone instead of getting a shorter list without knowing why.

Muse looks up missing album covers online. If you’d rather choose your own, upload one on the album page.

Now playing.

Open the player to see the cover up close, with the playback controls beside it. The colours on screen come from the cover of the record that’s playing, and change when the record does.

Up next shows the songs coming up. Drag a song to reorder it, or take it out of the queue. From any song’s menu you can choose Play next, Add to queue or Add to playlist.

Shuffle and repeat are in the player, and Muse remembers them, along with your queue, the next time you open it.

Follow the lyrics.

Muse looks up lyrics online and shows them beside the player. When the lyrics are synced, they scroll with the song, line by line.

When a song’s lyrics aren’t synced, you can time them yourself:

  • Tap along. In the lyrics panel, play the song and tap (or press Space) as each line starts. Muse saves the timings.
  • Auto-align. With smart features installed, Muse can time the lyrics to the audio for you. Choose it from a song’s menu or its lyrics panel.

To fix the words themselves, choose Edit lyrics from a song’s menu. If the song had synced timings, you choose whether to keep them or remove them.

Tell Muse
how you feel.

Muse keeps the music going by picking what plays next. Start it from a record or an artist, then choose a mood:

  • Chill
  • Late night
  • Focus
  • Feel good
  • Hype
  • In love
  • Heartbroken
  • Angry

Or fine-tune it yourself: set how happy, sad, calm, energetic, angry or romantic you want it. Muse picks songs from your library to match, starting from what’s on now. Each pick leads on from the one before, so an hour of listening travels somewhere instead of circling the song you started with.

Change the mood and Muse replaces the songs it picked. The songs you chose yourself stay where they are.

Skipping a track steers Muse away from songs like that one. Listening all the way through, or playing a song again, tells it you liked it.

Muse works without smart features, but it chooses much better with them: it follows how the music sounds and what the songs are about.

Smart features.

Smart features let Muse listen to each song and read its lyrics, on your computer, so it can choose better next songs. They also power Auto-align for lyrics.

  1. Go to Settings › Smart features and click Install. Muse also offers it in a banner.
  2. Muse downloads the models, about 3.6 GB. The exact size is shown before you start. If the download stops, it picks up where it left off.
  3. Muse then works through your library in the background. You can keep listening while it does.

Nothing leaves your computer except the download itself. Your music and lyrics are analysed on your computer.

Smart features need a Mac with Apple silicon or Windows. On Intel Macs, Muse plays and browses your music without them.

Editing your library.

Edits never change your music files. Muse keeps them in its own database.

  • Song details. Change a song’s title, artist, album, track number, disc number or year. Reset to file tags undoes your changes and reads the file again.
  • Remove. Hides a song from your library and keeps its play history. Plain Remove never deletes a file. Choose Also move to Trash to send the file to your system Trash too. Bring removed songs back in Settings › Removed songs.
  • Hide artist. On an artist page, takes the artist off the Artists list. Their albums and songs still show. Unhide them in Settings › Hidden artists.
  • Covers and artist pictures. Upload a JPEG, PNG or WebP image (up to 10 MB) on an album or artist page. Muse never replaces a cover you uploaded.

Tray, mini player
and drop.

  • Keep playing in the background. Closing the window hides Muse to the system tray on Windows, or the menu bar on macOS. The menu there has Play/Pause, Next, Mini player, Show Muse and Quit Muse.
  • Mini player. A small window that stays on top of everything else, with the cover, the title and the previous, play/pause and next buttons. Open it from the tray or menu bar.
  • Drag and drop. Drop a folder onto the Muse window to add it as a music folder. Drop a song to add it where it is: nothing is copied.

Updating Muse.

When a newer Muse is out, Settings › Advanced shows Update to Muse with the new version number. Muse downloads it, installs it and restarts, with no download page and no first-launch warning.

This works from Muse 0.1.5 on. On an earlier version, download the newest Muse and install it over the top. Every release is also on GitHub Releases.

Your data
and privacy.

Muse only reads your music folders. It never writes to them, unless you choose Move to Trash. Everything Muse saves goes in one folder of its own:

macOS
~/Library/Application Support/io.github.ianthemitten.muse
Windows
%LOCALAPPDATA%\io.github.ianthemitten.muse

That folder holds your library database (songs, plays and ratings), lyrics, covers and artist pictures, your settings, smart features once installed, and muse.log.

Muse has no account and sends your listening nowhere. It goes online only to look up covers and lyrics, to download smart features, and to check for updates.

If something
goes wrong.

macOS says Muse can’t be opened or is damaged

Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway next to the message about Muse. If macOS says Muse “is damaged and can’t be opened”, open Terminal, run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Muse.app, then open Muse again. Full first-launch steps.

Windows says it protected my PC

Click More info, then Run anyway. The warning appears because the installer isn’t signed yet, not because something harmful was found. Muse’s source code is public on GitHub.

Some of my songs don’t show up

Check that the folder is listed in Settings and that the files are MP3, M4A, FLAC, WAV, OGG or Opus. Also check Settings › Removed songs and Settings › Hidden artists.

The window stays blank, or Muse won’t start

When Muse can’t start, the start screen shows the last lines of muse.log and where it is. On Windows, Muse 0.1.6 fixed a blank window after “Starting Muse”: download the newest Muse and install it over the top. If it still happens, open an issue on GitHub with the log.

I can’t install smart features

Smart features need a Mac with Apple silicon or Windows. On an Intel Mac, Settings says they aren’t available there. Muse still plays, browses and picks songs without them.
